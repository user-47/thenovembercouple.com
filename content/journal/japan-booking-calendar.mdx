The single biggest lesson from planning Japan: **the best experiences don't sell out because they're expensive — they sell out because everyone is waiting for the same minute.** Here are the release rules we planned around. Every time is Japan time (JST), which is why our [trip planner](/trips/japan/planner) converts them to yours.

## The ones that sell out in minutes

- **Ghibli Museum** — the whole of next month goes on sale on the **10th at 10:00 AM JST**. Tickets are named, so bring matching ID.
- **Pokémon Café (Tokyo & Osaka)** — reservations open **31 days ahead at 6:00 PM JST**. Have the page open early and retry around 6:15 for cancellations.
- **Kirby Café** — next month opens on the **10th at 6:00 PM JST**.
- **Nintendo Museum** — a **lottery about three months ahead**, with any leftovers sold later.

## The ones to book the moment they open

- **Tokyo Disneyland & DisneySea** — dated tickets open **60 days ahead at 2:00 PM JST**. Under-4s go free.
- **Shibuya Sky** — web tickets open **two weeks ahead at midnight JST**. Sunset slots go first.
- **Universal Studios Japan** — Studio Passes and Express Passes release about **three months ahead**, one date at a time.

## The ones to book a few weeks out

teamLab Planets, the Harry Potter Studio Tour, kimono photoshoots and the Shinkansen (book 21+ days ahead on smartEX for the cheapest fares) don't have a single release moment, but good slots go weeks before.

Release rules do change — Shibuya Sky moved from four weeks to two in 2025 — so we date-stamp every activity in the planner with when we last checked it.
